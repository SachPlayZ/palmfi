# A Frozen Vault keeps paying known counterparties through the Orbital pool (Lifeline)

A freeze stops the attacker, but it also stops the business: no payroll, no suppliers, no customer withdrawals, while recovery takes days. In a real incident the treasurer is pushed to unfreeze early and take the risk, which undoes the protection. When Bybit lost about $1.5B in February 2025, other exchanges lent it emergency liquidity over the phone within hours. A Lifeline does the same thing onchain.

While a Vault is Frozen, any wallet may propose paying a payee Vault. It takes effect only with **both** the Recovery key and a majority of the wallets (snapshot at proposal). The payee must already be a counterparty from before the freeze: in the Vault's executed-outflow history (`destinationCount > 0`) or its onboarding destinations (`isDestination`). The vote approves a payment to that payee and never chooses a destination (ADR-0004 / ADR-0008). The proposer's Protected tokens for the amount plus a 0.5% fee are escrowed by burning, so the frozen USDC never moves. Outstanding Lifelines (pending plus drawn) are capped at 20% of the Vault's USDC.

When it is approved, the Vault calls `OrbitalHook.lifelineDraw`. The pool pays the payee's own token to the payee's first wallet out of the payee's pool float, records the debt, and caps the principal fronted against any payee token at 50% of that token's liquidity. On the first `settle` after the payer can settle again, whether unfrozen or Migrated, the hook has the payer Vault send principal plus fee in USDC to the payee Vault. The payee mints the fee to its wallets and the principal back into the pool. Migration keeps the debt back with the token supply, so it can't be dodged by migrating.

Uniswap is load-bearing here: the v4 hook holds the float that fronts the payment, enforces who may draw, and routes the repayment through settlement.

The cost: a payee whose float is fronted can't withdraw that part of its liquidity until repayment, and earns the fee for that wait. A debt to a payee that is itself Frozen or Migrated waits until that payee can receive again. If the Recovery key and a majority are both compromised, an attacker could pay known counterparties up to the cap. That is the same majority that can already migrate the Vault, and the money still reaches only payees the Vault had already paid.
